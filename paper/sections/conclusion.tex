\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present the first controlled cross-family transfer study in neural differential cryptanalysis, comparing ARX (\speck), Feistel (\simon), and SPN (\present) under identical experimental conditions.
The central finding---transfer polarity is family-dependent, with ARX and Feistel exhibiting anti-transfer (45.5\%, $p < 10^{-8}$) and SPN exhibiting positive transfer (99.1\%, $p < 10^{-11}$)---provides direct empirical evidence about the structural features these networks learn.
All results are obtained with MLP in the 2-1-CT-R setting; the ResNet comparison ($\leq$2.6\pp gap, ordering preserved) confirms the findings are architecture-independent.
Future work should test transfer polarity under stronger architectures (DBitNet, SENet), evolutionary input difference search, multi-pair aggregation, and on larger block sizes (SPECK64/128, SIMON64/128).
